Diagnosis of Shock

Shock = hypotension, tachycardia, tachypnea + signs of poor tissue perfusion

Poor tissue perfusion:

  • Cold clammy skin: delayed capillary refilling time (normal ≤ 2 seconds)
  • Alteration of consciousness, syncope
  • Bowel ileus (increase sympathetic response)
  • Oliguria (urine output < 0.5 mL/kg/hr or 100 – 400 mL/day)

Liver lab interpretation

Biochemistry

Aminotransferase interpretation

Mild elevation (< 5x): Chronic hepatic injury

  • Exogenous: virus, alcohol, drug
  • Endogenous: NAFLD, metabolic liver disease

Moderate elevation (> 5x): Acute hepatic injury

  • Exogenous: virus, drug
  • Endogenous: autoimmune hepatitis, Wilson’s disease, acute biliary tract obstruction

Markedly high elevation (> 40x)

  • Ischemic hepatitis
  • Toxic hepatitis (paracetamol)

Rapid declination within a week

  • Ischemic hepatic injury (decrease 50% per day)
  • Drug induced hepatitis: paracetamol (short half life)
  • Acute biliary tract obstruction
  • Fulminant hepatitis

Slow declination within several weeks

  • Acute viral hepatitis (decrease 10% per day)
  • Drug induced hepatitis: sulfa group (long half life)
  • Autoimmune disease
  • Metabolic liver disease

Acute hepatic injury (1-2 days):

  • AST > ALT
  • cytosolic enzyme release

Chronic hepatic injury, most types of liver disease:

  • ALT > AST (due to longer half-life)
  • Persistence abnormal ALT >= 6 months
  • Abnormal ALT > once in 6 months
  • cytosolic enzyme release

Acute alcohol-induced / fulminant hepatitis:

  • AST > ALT (AST:ALT ratio > 2)
  • AST,ALT level usually < 300 U/L
  • Mitochondrial damage -> ASTm release
  • Vit B6 deficient -> decrease ALT synthesis

Cirrhosis:

  • AST > ALT (AST:ALT ratio > 1)
  • Low albumin (< 3.5 g/dL)
  • Prolonged PT (INR > 1.3)
  • High bilirubin (> 1.5 mg/dL)
  • Portal hypertension
  • Splenomegaly (> 13 cm) -> hypersplenism -> thrombocytopenia
  • Dilated portal vein (> 13 mm)
  • Pulmonary shunt
  • Ascites
  • Hepatorenal syndrome
  • Esophageal/rectal varices, periumbilical caput medusae -> hemorrhage
  • Encephalopathy (from anemia)
  • Low plt., WBC, hemoglobin
  • Fibrosis progress -> decrease ALT production

Cholestasis:

  • elevated ALP, GGT
  • Suppression of bile flow -> retention of bile -> bile dissolve membrane fragments
  • GGT helps confirm hepatic origin of ALP elevation

R value = (ALT/upper normal limit of ALT) / (ALP/upper normal limit of ALP)

> 5       Hepatocellular pattern

2 – 5    Mixed pattern

< 2       Cholestatic pattern

Ascitic fluid analysis

Serum ascites albumin gradient (SAAG) = serum albumin - ascitic fluid albumin

 SAAG >= 1.1 g/dL -> reflect portal hypertension

  • Ascitic protein < 2.5 g/dL
  • Cirrhosis
  • Massive liver metastases
  • Ascitic protein >= 2.5 g/dL
  • Heart failure
  • Constrictive pericarditis
  • IVC obstruction
  • Sinusoidal obstruction syndrome

SAAG < 1.1 g/dL

  • Peritoneal carcinomatosis
  • Tuberculosis peritonitis
  • Nephrotic syndrome
  • Pancreatitis
  • Other clinical conditions

Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP)

  • Ascitic neutrophil >= 250 /mm^3 (gold standard)

Secondary peritonitis

  • Ascitic neutrophil > 250 /mm^3
  • Ascitic fluid: high protein, low glucose
  • Ascitic fluid culture: multiple organisms
