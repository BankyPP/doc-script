P32-34: Section 5: Vascular & head injury. Acute stroke: ischemic 70% vs hemorrhagic 30%. Acute ischemic stroke = occlusion of artery. Types: disease of blood vessels; cardioembolism; disease of blood components. Atherosclerosis LARGE vessel: 1. rupture of plaque -> thrombus formation (platelet) -> no blood flow; or artery-to-artery emboli (หลุด, occlude distal). 2. disruption of flow from fixed stenosis (mismatch demand & supply) -> low flow.
Global ischemia (hypotension, shock, cardiac arrest): selective vulnerability of neurons (ตายไม่พร้อมกัน; ใช้ออกซิเจนเยอะ): CA1 hippocampus (memory), cerebellar Purkinje (coordination), medium-sized striatal neurons, pyramidal neurons cortical layers 3,5,6. Watershed (border zone) infarction (อยู่ปลายทาง): between MCA-ACA, MCA-PCA: bilateral arm weakness ("man in a barrel"), aphasia (transcortical).
P35: Clinical acute stroke: SUDDEN onset neuro deficit explained by lesion in vascular territory. Small vessel disease: occlusion of perforator artery -> lacunar infarction (lipohyalinosis; หลอดเลือดเสื่อม/อุด จาก HT); common locations: basal ganglia, internal capsule, thalamus, pons, cerebellum. Penumbra vs ischemic core; time passes core bigger; TIME IS BRAIN: 1 min = 1.9 million neurons & 14 billion synapses -> stroke fast track.
P36 (thumbnails): Reperfusion: thrombolytic agents <4.5 h; mechanical thrombectomy <24 h (large vessels, stroke imaging e.g. CT perfusion mismatch; endovascular, ใส่สายเข้าไปลากลิ่มเลือดออก); stroke unit >24 h (admit). MUST EXCLUDE*** before rtPA: ICH (CT non-contrast; hyperdensity white), stroke mimics (hypoglycemia, seizure, migraine), large infarction (เนื้อสมองตายไปเยอะ).
BP acute: thrombolytics before target <185/110, after <180/105; if BP > target -> anti-HTN. No thrombolysis: if SBP<220 & DBP<120 NO anti-HTN med (except other HTN emergencies: CHF, aortic dissection, AMI, AKI, HT encephalopathy); SBP>220 or DBP>120 consider anti-HTN (ลด ~15% in first 24h). Section 8 Pain & headache. Types of pain: nociceptive (somatic, visceral, referred), neuropathic.
P60: Pain processing: transduction (nociceptor) -> transmission (pain afferent -> dorsal horn -> spinothalamic tract) -> modulation (descending; 5HT, NE) -> perception (cortex). Withdrawal reflex. Pain afferents: Aδ fiber (faster; 1st pain sharp) vs C fiber (slower; 2nd pain dull/burning) = double pain sensation. Pain transmission: free nerve endings -> DRG (1st) -> dorsal horn (2nd) -> spinothalamic -> thalamus (3rd); face: trigeminal ganglion (1st) -> trigeminal nucleus caudalis (2nd) -> trigeminothalamic -> thalamus.
Terminology: peripheral sensitization (↑responsiveness, ↓threshold of peripheral nociceptors ~ maladaptive); central sensitization (↑responsiveness of central nociceptive neurons to normal/subthreshold input; แปลผลเกิน); hyperalgesia (↑response to noxious stimulus; เจ็บ 3 -> 7); allodynia (pain from stimulus that normally doesn't trigger nociceptor; ลมพัดก็เจ็บ) — from peripheral/central sensitization.
P61 Migraine pathophysiology: cortical spreading depression (spreading depolarization -> hyperpolarization wave) starts occipital cortex (visual aura #1) / somatosensory cortex at parietal (#2); ↑extracellular K+, H+, NO, AA, glu -> activate pial nociceptors (trigeminal) -> TG (1st order) -> TNC/TCC (trigeminal nucleus caudalis / trigeminocervical complex, brainstem, 2nd) -> thalamus (3rd) -> cortex. Release CGRP, PACAP, SP, NK -> vasodilation, neurogenic inflammation, axon reflex to meningeal vessels at dura (cycle) -> peripheral sensitization (TG; throbbing pain ↑) -> central sensitization (TNC; allodynia). Trigeminal autonomic reflex: TNC -> SSN (superior salivatory nucleus) -> SPG (sphenopalatine ganglion) parasympathetic -> VIP, NO, NPY vessel dilate (lacrimation, rhinorrhea).
P62: Pain modulation: gate control theory & descending pathway. Gate control (AC neuro): C fiber (nociceptive) inhibits inhibitory interneuron (−) -> pain to spinothalamic; Aβ fiber (non-nociceptive, touch) excites inhibitory interneuron (+) -> closes gate (e.g. ทายาถูๆ นวด -> หายปวด; adapt). Descending: PAG -> opioids; nucleus raphe magnus, dorsal raphe -> 5-HT; locus coeruleus -> NE; descending fibers suppress noxious input at 2nd order neurons (ตัวลด pain).
P63: Headache classification: primary (migraine*, TTH, TACs e.g. cluster headache, others); secondary (traumatic, vascular, non-vascular, substance-related, infectious, homeostatic, paracranial, psychogenic); cranial neuralgias (trigeminal, glossopharyngeal, occipital). Migraine: orbital-temporal-occipital; unilateral (alternating; ข้างเดียวเด่น สลับได้) or bilateral; throbbing/pulsating (ตุ้บๆ vessel); episodic 4-72 h; N/V; photophobia/phonophobia. Phases: prodrome (premonitory, hours-days) -> aura (5-60 min) -> attack (4-72 h) -> resolution/postdrome (1-2 days).
P64: Migraine with aura ~30%. Pathophys summary: hypothalamic dysfunction (migraine generator), serotonin depletion, CSD, trigeminal nociceptive activation, meningeal vasodilation/neurogenic inflammation/peripheral sensitization, central sensitization, CGRP hypothesis*. Relievers: non-specific paracetamol, NSAIDs/COX-2 (↓inflammation), opioids; migraine-specific: ergotamine (limb ischemia), triptans (5-HT1B/1D agonist; 1B = blood vessels -> vasoconstriction; 1D -> inhibit neuropeptide release/central pain pathway), ditans (5-HT1F agonist; lasmiditan - no vasoconstriction; slide says 5-HT1D? use 1F). Abortive (acute; give before central sensitization) vs preventive (prophylaxis; controller).

P14 (sec 3-4 thumbnails extra): "Hemiparesis" almost always = CNS lesion at corticospinal tract (contralateral). Corticobulbar tract to facial nucleus (UMN facial: forehead spared). Pyramidal weakness: UL extensors weaker than flexors; LL flexors weaker than extensors.
Rigidity vs spasticity: rigidity = velocity independent, no clasp-knife, extrapyramidal lesion, no definite pattern (lead-pipe/cogwheel); spasticity = velocity dependent, clasp-knife, pyramidal lesion, pyramidal pattern (UL flexors, LL extensors).
Roles of cerebellum: integrator of visual (conscious), proprioceptive & vestibular (unconscious) input -> extrapyramidal motor control; loss of 1 input compensated by others.

Vertigo: true vertigo? central or peripheral. Peripheral (inner ear, vestibular nerve): position-related (stereotypic), ± hearing abnormality, absence CNS signs; more severe vertigo, intense N/V, unidirectional horizontal nystagmus. Central (vestibular nuclei, brainstem connecting tracts, cerebellum; stroke): not related to head position, no hearing abnormality, presence of CNS manifestations; vestibular ataxia milder.
Ataxia: cerebellar (wide-based gait, dysmetria, poor coordination, scanning speech, truncal ataxia); sensory (worsens when cutting off visual input; Romberg +); vestibular (with vertigo, N/V, oscillopsia).

Cranial nerve exam: papilledema; light reflex (afferent CN2, efferent CN3); RAPD (Marcus Gunn); EOM; facial weakness: UMN (forehead spared, lower face contralateral) vs LMN (whole half face, ipsilateral; Bell palsy); tongue deviates toward weak side (CN12). P16 (investigations & consciousness): Head trauma (MCA accident, transient LOC, contusion) -> CT brain (acute bleeding). Sudden L hemiparesis 90 min, CT normal -> MRI brain (DWI) to confirm ischemic stroke. Neurological investigations: neuroimaging, LP & CSF, EEG, electrodiagnosis (NCS, RNS, EMG), blood tests (immune, neurodegenerative biomarkers).
LP: needle passes skin, subQ, supraspinous & interspinous ligament, ligamentum flavum (last structure before epidural; "pop"), (epidural space), dura mater, arachnoid -> CSF (L3-4/L4-5). Indications: diagnostic: meningitis (infectious, carcinomatous, lymphomatous), SAH (if imaging cannot confirm), neurodegenerative biomarkers (AD), prion (CJD); therapeutic: CSF drainage (IIH, NPH), intrathecal drugs. Contraindications: risk of brain herniation (focal deficit, ↑ICP, mass with midline shift, obstructive hydrocephalus 4th ventricle outlet), skin infection at site, coagulopathy, cardiorespiratory compromise. Brain imaging (CT) before LP if: focal neuro deficits, ↑ICP (papilledema), altered consciousness, immunocompromised, seizure, other clues of space-occupying lesion. Complications: post-LP headache (CSF leak; most common), brain herniation (most serious), local/referred pain, cardiorespiratory compromise, bleeding, infection, epidermoid cyst.
SAH vs traumatic tap: SAH -> uniformly bloody all tubes, xanthochromia (pink oxyHb, yellow bilirubin several hours (6-12h) after bleed); traumatic tap -> clearing in successive tubes, no xanthochromia. Other xanthochromia: severe hyperbilirubinemia, very high CSF protein, metastatic melanoma.
EEG benefits: diagnose seizure disorder (if not sure), classify seizure, localize epileptogenic focus, sleep-related events, monitor brain function during vascular surgery (carotid), prognosis in comatose patient, alternative confirmation of brain death.
NCS: sensory NCV = distance/latency; motor NCV = distance between stimuli / (latency2 - latency1). Prominent ↓amplitude = axonopathy; prominent ↓NCV (slowing, prolonged latency) = demyelination. NCS pattern: mononeuropathy, mononeuropathy multiplex, polyneuropathy. RNS: decremental at low rate 3-5 Hz = MG; incremental at high rate 20-50 Hz = LEMS. EMG: surface vs needle (more accurate). Myopathy: small-amplitude, short-duration, polyphasic MUAPs (early recruitment); neuropathy (denervation/reinnervation): large-amplitude, long-duration giant MUAPs, reduced recruitment; fibrillations. Myotome EMG localizes nerve vs root (e.g. wrist drop radial n. vs C7 radiculopathy; foot drop peroneal n. vs L5 radiculopathy).
Consciousness two dimensions: arousal/wakefulness (level; ARAS brainstem) & awareness (content; cerebral cortex). States: coma (no arousal no awareness), vegetative state (arousal without awareness), minimally conscious, locked-in (full), delirium (acute confusional; awareness fluctuates), REM sleep. ARAS: reticular formation (midbrain) -> diencephalon (thalamus, hypothalamus) -> diffuse cortex; lesions altered consciousness: bilateral cortex/diencephalon or brainstem ARAS. Quiz: unilateral lesion like large right frontal lobe tumor without shift does NOT cause altered LOC; pontine hemorrhage (ARAS) and large MCA stroke with shift do.
GCS (3-15): Eye 4 (spontaneous 4, to sound 3, to pressure 2, none 1); Verbal 5 (oriented 5, confused 4, words 3, sounds 2, none 1); Motor 6 (obeys 6, localizes 5, normal flexion/withdrawal 4, abnormal flexion (decorticate) 3, extension (decerebrate) 2, none 1). Quiz: eyes closed never open E1, incomprehensible sounds V2, localize sometimes M5 -> E1V2M5 (=8). Decorticate posture (abnormal flexion; lesion above red nucleus) vs decerebrate (extension; lesion below red nucleus, midbrain/pons).
Primary position of eyes: midline; conjugate deviation (FEF or PPRF); downward deviation (thalamus/dorsal midbrain); unconjugated / skew deviation -> brainstem (exception: pre-existing strabismus, CN palsy).
Clinical approach altered consciousness: step 1 confirm, step 2 anatomical localization (brainstem signs: pupils, doll's eye, skew), step 3 etiology: brainstem lesion (vascular, inflammatory, neoplastic) vs diffuse cortical (vascular large, inflammatory e.g. encephalitis/meningitis, neoplastic w ↑ICP, epileptic (NCSE), toxic/metabolic encephalopathy). Metabolic encephalopathy causes: dysglycemia, cerebral anoxia, CO2 narcosis, electrolyte (hypo/hyperNa, hyperCa), uremic, hepatic, myxedema coma, hypertensive encephalopathy, nutritional (Wernicke). Clinical clues: presence of roving eye balls, asterixis (flapping, frog legs), full-blown systemic disorder, fluctuation; absence of focal deficit, long tract sign, brainstem sign.
Quizzes: 5) uncontrolled HT, sudden LOC, horizontal dysconjugate eyes, decerebrate, doll's eye negative -> brainstem lesion -> CT brain. 6) homeless comatose, normal vitals, no focal -> blood sugar DTX first. 7) fever, headache, progressive ↓LOC 1 wk, absent DTR LL, BBK + both -> do NOT do LP first (CT first). 8) cirrhotic confusion -> hepatic encephalopathy mechanism (↑ammonia).
Brain death: coma due to irreversible structural brain damage (exclude reversible: intoxication, severe hypothermia, endocrine/metabolic, shock); absence of all brainstem reflexes (pupils, corneal, VOR, doll's eyes, gag/cough) and spontaneous movement for 6 consecutive hours; positive apnea test; assessed by ≥3 physicians.
Syncope: transient global cerebral hypoperfusion (ARAS/cortex) -> TLOC. 3 types: reflex (vasovagal), orthostatic hypotension (dehydration, autonomic dysfunction; supine->upright BP drop), cardiac (aortic stenosis, arrhythmia; dangerous). Reflex syncope: predisposing (dehydration, peripheral vasodilation) -> ↓venous return -> baroreceptor reflex ↑sym ↓parasym; "empty ventricle" vigorous contraction stimulates ventricular mechanoreceptors (Bezold-Jarisch) -> paradoxical ↓sym ↑parasym -> bradycardia + vasodilation -> syncope; fear/anxiety trigger. Symptoms: transient LOC (seconds, max 1-2 min), cold pale skin, possible brief clonic jerks/urinary incontinence; presyncope (lightheadedness, palpitation, visual disturbance); rapid recovery without deficits. Syncope vs seizure table (duration, tonic-clonic, tongue biting lateral = seizure, postictal confusion, muscle aching after).
P17: SWS (port-wine stain V1, tram-track calcification). Neurulation: ectoderm -> neural plate -> neural tube; neural crest; notochord. NTDs: open (no skin cover: myelomeningocele, anencephaly) vs closed (skin covered: spina bifida occulta, lipomeningocele); meningocele (meninges+CSF) vs myelomeningocele (+ cord); environmental (folate deficiency, maternal DM, obesity, valproate/carbamazepine) & genetic factors; folic acid prevention.
Migraine: orbital-temporal-occipital, unilateral alternating/bilateral, throbbing, episodic 4-72h, N/V, photophobia/phonophobia (osmophobia). 4 phases: premonitory (hours-days), aura (5-60 min), headache (4-72 h), resolution (1-2 days). Pathophys: hypothalamic disturbances (generator) + CSD (aura) + inadequate pain modulation -> activation of trigeminal nociceptive system -> neurogenic inflammation of meningeal vessels & peripheral sensitization -> central sensitization. Descending modulation: PAG (endogenous opioids), LC (NE), raphe nuclei (5-HT) -> TCC/TNC dorsal horn.
Preventive mechanism: β-blockers (central sensitization?, inhibit iNOS, ↓cortical excitability), ASM (block excitatory channels, facilitate GABA), TCA (↑synaptic 5HT & NE; α2), SNRI, CCB (block excitatory channels), ARB/ACEI, BoNT (destroys SNARE; chronic migraine), CGRP mAbs / gepants (inhibit pain sensitization peripherally).
Cluster headache: episodic clusters; unilateral orbital pain + autonomic (lacrimation, rhinorrhea, ptosis, miosis), restless; circadian (same time, night); 100% oxygen. TTH: band-like around temples ± neck/shoulder; worse afternoon-evening of working days; mild-moderate; paracetamol/NSAIDs/COX-2; if migraine co-exists -> TCA.
Dementia cases: Case 1 (70F, 5 yr progressive recent memory loss, hippocampus, then parietal (apraxia), frontal) -> Alzheimer's disease (moderate) — chronic progressive cognitive decline with impaired IADL. Case 2 (54M 1 yr behavior change: disinhibition, apathy, loss of empathy, hyperorality, compulsive; frontal/orbitofrontal) -> frontotemporal dementia (bvFTD; frontal then temporal; tau/TDP-43 not amyloid). Case 3 (75M 6 mo gait disturbance (magnetic gait, pivot turning), psychomotor slowness, urinary incontinence; HT, smoking) -> NPH triad (gait apraxia, psychomotor slowness/dementia, urinary incontinence); imaging communicating hydrocephalus, DESH (disproportionately enlarged subarachnoid space hydrocephalus, crowding of vertex); "dementia with potential reversibility" — CSF tap test, VP shunt. Case 4 (60F sudden loss of new memory for hours, repetitive questioning, otherwise intact, resolves <24h) -> transient global amnesia (TGA): anterograde (+ some retrograde) amnesia; hippocampus (CA1) — episodic memory (Papez circuit); DWI punctate hippocampal lesions; benign, rarely recurs.
CNS infections: bacterial meningitis (acute pyogenic: E. coli, GBS, S. pneumoniae, N. meningitidis, L. monocytogenes); chronic meningitis (TB); brain abscess (polymicrobial strep, staph, G- bacilli, anaerobes); viral: aseptic meningitis (enteroviruses, influenza), encephalitis (HSV, EV71, JE, JC, dengue), acute flaccid myelitis (poliovirus); fungal chronic meningitis (Cryptococcus neoformans, Candida); parasitic: amebic encephalitis (Balamuthia, Acanthamoeba, Naegleria), brain abscess (Toxoplasma), cysticercosis (Taenia solium).
Pathogens by age: <1 mo GBS (S. agalactiae), E. coli, Listeria; 1-23 mo GBS, E. coli, H. influenzae type b, S. pneumoniae, N. meningitidis (non-typhoidal Salmonella); 2-50 yr S. pneumoniae, N. meningitidis; >60 yr (>50) S. pneumoniae, N. meningitidis, Listeria. Mnemonic "Some Killers Have Pretty Nice Capsules" (encapsulated: Strep pneumo, Klebsiella, H. influenzae b, Pseudomonas, N. meningitidis, Cryptococcus) -> asplenia. Predisposing: asplenia (encapsulated S. pneumo, Hib, N. meningitidis); complement deficiency terminal (MAC) -> N. meningitidis; cell-mediated immunity defect (HIV, steroid) -> Listeria, non-typhoidal Salmonella; mother-to-child -> GBS, Listeria; base of skull fracture/CSF leak -> S. pneumoniae; head trauma/neurosurgery -> S. aureus, CoNS, aerobic G- incl Pseudomonas; CSF shunt -> CoNS, S. aureus, P. aeruginosa, Cutibacterium acnes; undercooked pig/pig farming/pork butchery -> Streptococcus suis (sensorineural hearing loss!); contaminated food (cold meat, vegetables, unpasteurized milk & cheese) -> Listeria.
Pathogenesis steps: I mucosal colonization; II intravascular survival (evade opsonophagocytosis/complement: polysaccharide capsule, pneumococcal surface proteins, factor H-binding protein (Neisseria)); intracellular survival (Listeria: cell-mediated immunity primary defense); III meningeal invasion (transcellular, paracellular, Trojan horse (leukocyte; Listeria, TB), contiguous spread, direct via nerve endings (olfactory/trigeminal: Burkholderia pseudomallei)); IV host immune response in SAS -> inflammation, edema, ↑ICP.
Clinical: classic triad fever, nuchal rigidity, altered mental status (only 2/3); fever >80%, meningismus 5-75%, AMS >75%, headache >85%, vomiting 35%, seizures 30%, focal deficits 10-35%, papilledema <5%; Kernig & Brudzinski (low sensitivity; absence does not rule out). N. meningitidis: droplet, close contact; septicemia/meningococcemia (40%), meningitis (50%) or both; purpura fulminans, rapid deterioration within hours (DIC); chemoprophylaxis for close contacts; vaccine-preventable. Listeria: foodborne / mother-to-fetus; invasive listeriosis: bacteremia, meningitis, rhombencephalitis (brainstem), brain abscess, endocarditis; neonates: abortion, prematurity, granulomatosis infantiseptica, sepsis, late-onset meningitis; immunocompetent: gastroenteritis self-limited.
Investigations: hemoculture (always before antibiotic; 50-80% positive), LP (CSF analysis; most useful if no contraindication; before antibiotic), brain imaging (diffuse meningeal enhancement not diagnostic; CT before LP if suspected mass/↑ICP: papilledema, focal deficit, ↓consciousness, new seizure, immunocompromised, CNS disease). DON'T DELAY ANTIBIOTIC (golden period). CSF acute bacterial meningitis: OP >180 mmH2O; WBC 10-10,000 neutrophils; RBC absent (non-traumatic); glucose <2.2 mmol/L (<40 mg/dl); CSF/serum glucose <0.4; protein >0.45 g/L; Gram stain + >60%; culture + >80%; PCR / 16S rRNA. Antibiotic a few hours before LP won't significantly alter CSF WBC & glucose but ↓culture yield. CSF table: viral glucose normal; Listeria may be monocytic; TB lymphocytes, protein 100-500 (high), glucose low, AFB <30%, PCR highest yield; cryptococcal India ink <50%, Crypto Ag (+ve 90%), very high OP.
Empirical antibiotics (by age): ceftriaxone/cefotaxime ± vancomycin (pneumococcal resistance) + ampicillin (if Listeria risk: neonate <1 mo, >50 yr, immunocompromised). Adjunctive dexamethasone 0.15 mg/kg q6h x 4 days, given 20 min before or with first antibiotic dose: ↓inflammation, ↓mortality & sensorineural hearing loss in S. pneumoniae (adults) & Hib (children); cons ↓vancomycin penetration.
TB meningitis: M. tuberculosis aerobic acid-fast bacilli, mycolic acid, slow growth (15-20 h); airborne; incubation 2-6 months after infection; high prevalence areas mostly children <5 yr; adults with HIV; ~50% accompanied by miliary TB; BCG 52-100% effective vs TBM & miliary. Clinical subacute-chronic (5-30 days): nonspecific prodrome weeks (low-grade fever, malaise, anorexia, irritability) -> meningeal signs, AMS, stroke, hydrocephalus, cranial nerve palsies (CN6). CT 4 patterns: basilar arachnoiditis (basal meningeal enhancement), tuberculoma, cerebral infarction (vasculitis), hydrocephalus. Diagnosis: symptoms + radiology/pathology + history TB exposure / positive TST or IGRA + microbiology (molecular/culture). Treatment: 2HRZE/10HR (12 months); alternative age 0-19 6HRZEto (ethionamide); adjunctive steroid dexamethasone or prednisolone tapered 6-8 wks (↓inflammation, brain edema, ↑survival; paradoxical reaction); manage ↑ICP (repeat tapping, VP shunt).
Brain abscess: intact parenchyma resistant; preexisting hypoxia/ischemia/necrosis needed. Stages early cerebritis (d1-3), late cerebritis (d4-9), early capsule (d10-13), late capsule (>d14). Routes: contiguous spread (~50%; middle ear, mastoid, sinuses (frontal), dental) — usually single; hematogenous (~30%; lung, heart (endocarditis), abdomen, pelvic, skin; cyanotic congenital heart dz R->L shunt (TOF)) — multiple, MCA territory, gray-white junction; post neurosurgery/head trauma (~10%); cryptogenic ~25%. Pathogens immunocompetent: streptococci (viridans 40%), Enterobacteriaceae (20%), anaerobes (30%), S. aureus (10%); immunocompromised: Nocardia, Toxoplasma, Candida, Cryptococcus, Aspergillus. Clinical triad fever + headache + focal deficit (<50%); time to hospital ~10 days; complications hydrocephalus, ventriculitis (rupture), ↑ICP, herniation, status epilepticus. Ix: CT/MRI with contrast (ring enhancement; DWI restricted); obtain abscess material & underlying focus (stain, culture, nucleic acid); hemoculture (10-30%), leukocytosis 50%, ESR/CRP, echo, CXR, dental exam; NEVER LUMBAR PUNCTURE (herniation risk, low yield). Treatment: antimicrobial ASAP (high-dose IV 6-8 wks then oral 2-3 mo; empirical ceftriaxone/cefotaxime + metronidazole) + neurosurgical (abscess >2.5 cm, symptomatic edema, ventricular rupture risk, accessible).
