Key concepts in lipid profiles

  • LDL = mainly cholesterol
  • VLDL = mainly triglyceride (TG)

  • ApoB = Bad lipid → if high: atherosclerosis risk ↑

  • LDL
  • VLDL, IDL, chylomicron

  • ApoA = Good lipid → if low: atherosclerosis risk ↑

  • HDL

  • Normal levels

  • Total cholesterol < 200
  • TG < 150
  • HDL > 40

  • Calculated LDL = total cholesterol - HDL - (TG/5)

Drugs (NLEM)

  • LDL-lowering → use when cholesterol ↑

  1. HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors (statins)

  • 1st line drugs in treatment & primary and secondary prevention

  • Mechanisms

  • Main mechanisms: decrease LDL level
  • Inhibit cholesterol synthesis at rate-limiting step
  • Increase LDL-receptor activity at liver
  • Other mechanisms:
  • Lowers TG level by decreasing VLDL production
  • Increases HDL level by increasing apo-A production

  • Guidelines & Clinical uses

  • High intensity drugs (most effective): LDL reduction ≥ 50%
  • Rosuvastatin
  • Atorvastatin
  • For short half-life drugs: administered in the evening

  • ADR, DI, contraindications

  • Simvastatin is metabolized by CYP3A4 at liver:
  • Contraindicated with CYP3A4 inhibitors
  • CYP3A4 inhibitors: amiodarone, antifungals (-azole), macrolides (-thromycin), CCB (-dipine), HIV drugs
  • Risk of statin-associated muscle symptoms (SAMS)
  • Myalgia, myopathies, rhabdomyolysis (listed in ascending order of severity)
  • Need to measure creatine kinase (CK: a muscle enzyme) before therapy or if symptoms develop
  • Contraindicated with gemfibrozil (induce myopathies by inhibiting hepatic uptake of statins, especially simvastatin)
  • Use fenofibrate instead of gemfibrozil
  • Mild elevation of liver enzymes
  • Should measure AST/ALT before therapy
  • Contraindicated in liver failure and cirrhosis
  • Risk of type 2 DM
  • Contraindicated in pregnancy

  1. Ezetimibe

  • Mechanisms

  • Main mechanisms: decrease LDL level
  • Inhibit cholesterol absorption at small intestine by inhibiting NPC1L1 (cholesterol transporter)
  • Reduced hepatic cholesterol → Increase LDL-receptor activity at liver
  • Do not inhibit cholesterol synthesis

  • Guidelines & Clinical uses (same as cholestyramine)

  • Add-on to statin therapy
  • In case maximum statin effects are still not enough
  • In case statins are contraindicated

  • ADR, DI, contraindications

  • ADR: statin-associated (myopathies)

  1. Cholestyramine: bile acid sequestrants (BAS)

  • Mechanisms

  • Main mechanisms: decrease LDL level by inhibiting bile acid
  • Binds to bile acids → Inhibit bile acid reabsorption
  • Convert cholesterol into bile
  • Reduced hepatic cholesterol → Increase LDL-receptor activity at liver
  • Other mechanisms:
  • Decrease plasma glucose

  • Guidelines & Clinical uses (same as ezetimibe)

  • Add-on to statin therapy
  • In case maximum statin effects are still not enough
  • In case statins are contraindicated

  • ADR, DI, contraindications

  • Increase plasma TG (Contraindicated if TG > 500)
  • Decrease absorption of fat-soluble vitamins & drugs
  • Drugs affected: statins, ezetimibe, fibrates, etc.
  • BAS should be administered 4 hours before other drugs (or 1 hour after)
  • GI side effects: constipation, nausea, bloating, abdominal pain
  • ADR: statin-associated

  • VLDL-lowering → use when TG ↑ (hypertriglyceridemia)

  1. Fibrates (Gemfibrozil, Fenofibrate)

  • Mechanisms

  • Main mechanisms: decrease TG level
  • Binds to PPARα → increase lipid metabolism
  • Increase LPL activity (LPL: lipoprotein lipase)
  • Increase HDL level by increasing production of apo-A

  • Guidelines & Clinical uses: treat hypertriglyceridemia

  • ADR, DI, contraindications

  • Gemfibrozil increases statins level in the plasma, especially simvastatin
  • Increased risk of myopathies
  • Risk of cholesterol gallstones
  • Caution in patients with low GFR (need dose adjustment or contraindicated)

  1. Omega-3 fatty acids: EPA & DHA (fish oil)

  • Full names

  • EPA: Eicosapentanoic acid
  • DHA: Docosahexaenoic acid

  • Mechanisms

  • Main mechanisms: decrease TG level
  • Decrease VLDL synthesis
  • Other mechanisms: overall CVD risk ↓
  • ↓ Decrease platelet aggregation
  • ↓ Inflammation
  • ↑ Vasodilation
  • Plaque-stabilizing

  • Guidelines & Clinical uses: treat hypertriglyceridemia

  • ADR, DI, contraindications: non-significant

Clinical Management

  • High cholesterol (high LDL)

  • Goal: reduce LDL-C level
  • Statins is the drug of choice
  • Add-on drugs: ezetimibe and BAS
  • In case maximum statin effects, yet still cannot reach the goal
  • In case statins are contraindicated
  • DM → metformin

  • Hypertriglyceridemia (high VLDL)

  • High TG level = 150 - 499 mg/dL

  • Goal: reduce VLDL level to reduce risk for ASCVD
  • Statins is the 1st line drug
  • If high risk or persistent high TG: add ω-3 fatty acids

  • Severe TG level ≥ 500

  • Increased risk of pancreatitis
  • Fibrates is the first drug of choice
  • Add-on: ω-3 fatty acids
  • Contraindicate: BAS
